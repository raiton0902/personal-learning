# Research basis and limits

These studies inform the design. They do not validate this skill or prove identical effects for all subjects, ages, and learning tasks.

## Retrieve even material previously answered correctly

Karpicke & Roediger (2008), *The Critical Importance of Retrieval for Learning*, Science, DOI: [10.1126/science.1152408](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1152408). In a vocabulary experiment, repeated retrieval after an item was first answered correctly improved delayed retention. Design implication: do not retire a concept because the first explanation seemed easy. This study alone does not establish how to teach geometry or art.

## Repeat learning across sessions

Rawson & Dunlosky (2011), *Optimizing Schedules of Retrieval Practice for Durable and Efficient Learning: How Much Is Enough?*, DOI: [10.1037/a0023956](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0023956). [ERIC record with author abstract](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ934616). Three experiments with conceptual material studied initial recall criteria and later relearning sessions. Relearning benefited long-term retention. Design implication: use delayed evidence, not only repeated correct answers in the same chat. No universal numerical schedule is claimed.

## Mix mathematical strategies

Rohrer, Dedrick, Hartwig & Cheung (2020), *A Randomized Controlled Trial of Interleaved Mathematics Practice*, DOI: [10.1037/edu0000367](https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000367). A preregistered classroom trial involving 54 seventh-grade classes compared interleaved and blocked practice. Design implication: mixed problems can practice choosing a method. This does not imply uniform random sampling over every school subject is optimal.

Rohrer, Dedrick & Stershic (2015), *Interleaved Practice Improves Mathematics Learning*, DOI: [10.1037/edu0000001](https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000001), [author manuscript](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED557355.pdf). Classroom mathematics practice with identical problems arranged differently favored interleaving on delayed tests. Use mixed review after relevant methods have been taught; a mixed set must still be solvable.

## What is engineering judgment here

Weighted random selection, a three-lapse relearning trigger, review count defaults, interest-based example selection, and step-size adjustment rules are prototype design choices. The cited studies do not establish their exact settings. Evaluate them against independent delayed performance, transfer, learner burden, and curriculum coverage. Repeated questions can reflect curiosity or a poor explanation; diagnose before assigning fragility. Maintain stronger topics through delayed checks while giving additional support to demonstrated gaps.
